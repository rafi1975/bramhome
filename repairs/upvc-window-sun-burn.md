# uPVC window frame: sun-reflection burn repair

Damage caused by a concentrated sunlight reflection (e.g. a mirror left on the sill). Typical result: a small melted pit with a charred centre and a darker heat halo on cream / beige–yellow woodgrain uPVC (not pure white).

## Frame colour (this job)

- Base colour: **beige–yellow / cream / ivory** with a subtle embossed woodgrain
- Do **not** use standard “brilliant white” uPVC paint — it will read as a bright patch
- Shop for labels such as: cream, ivory, beige, magnolia, or the maker’s colour code if you still have paperwork
- Bring a clear daylight photo (or a clean undamaged corner) when buying paint or ordering a bead

The yellow ring around the burn is **heat scorch**, not the true frame colour. Match the clean beige nearby, not the scorched halo.

## What you’re looking at

| Feature | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| Dark, cratered centre | Plastic melted and carbonised |
| Yellow / orange ring | Heat discoloured the uPVC without full melt (ignore this when colour-matching) |
| Size ~1×3 cm, localised | Cosmetic / shallow structural — not a whole-frame failure |

**Important:** Confirm whether the burn is on the **removable glazing bead** (the strip that holds the glass) or on the **fixed frame**. Beads are cheap to replace; fixed-frame damage is usually filled and painted.

How to check: the bead is usually a separate clip-in strip around the glass. Gently probe the joint — if that piece can come out as a unit, prefer **replacement** over filling.

---

## Option A — Replace the glazing bead (best if damage is on the bead)

1. Measure the damaged bead length and note the profile (square, sculptured, etc.) and **cream/beige woodgrain** finish.
2. Order a matching uPVC glazing bead from the window maker or a local fabricator. Bring a short offcut or clear photos if unsure — cream shades vary a lot between brands.
3. Remove the old bead carefully (plastic packing knife / bead removal tool) so you don’t crack the glass or gasket.
4. Refit the new bead and reseat the gasket so the glass is held evenly.

Cost is usually low; factory colour/woodgrain match is better than filler + paint on cream frames.

---

## Option B — Fill and finish (fixed frame, or bead not replaceable)

Use this when the burn is on the main frame or you cannot get a matching bead quickly.

### Materials

- Degreaser or isopropyl alcohol
- Fine abrasive: 180 → 320 → 400 grit (wet/dry)
- uPVC repair filler / two-part plastic filler rated for PVC (not ordinary wall filler)
- Optional: uPVC welding rod + hot-air tool (advanced; only if you know the process)
- **Cream / beige / ivory** uPVC repair paint, touch-up stick, or aerosol — same sheen as the frame (usually satin / low-sheen, not gloss)
- Optional: small tubes of tint (yellow/ochre/brown) if you need to nudge a near-match cream warmer or darker
- Masking tape, lint-free cloths, disposable gloves

### Steps

1. **Clean**  
   Wipe the area with degreaser/IPA. Remove dust and grease so filler bonds.

2. **Remove loose char**  
   With a sharp craft knife or fine scraper, cut away soft, black, crumbly plastic until you reach firm material. Don’t dig deep into chambers if you can avoid it — stop when the remaining surface is solid.

3. **Key the surface**  
   Lightly sand the pit and a small halo around it (about 1–2 cm) with 180 grit so the filler has grip. Wipe clean. Sand enough of the scorched yellow ring that clean cream shows at the edge — otherwise paint will sit on discoloured plastic and look patchy.

4. **Fill**  
   Mix and press uPVC/plastic filler into the void proud of the surface. Work it into undercuts. Let it cure fully per the product instructions (often several hours).

5. **Shape**  
   Sand flush with the surrounding profile (180 → 320 → 400). Follow the slope of the frame/bead — the eye notices flat spots. Recoat with a thin skim of filler if there are pinholes, then sand again.

6. **Colour match (cream/beige)**  
   - Mask glass and gaskets.  
   - Test the paint on a scrap or tape first; compare in **daylight** against an undamaged part of the same frame (not under warm bulbs).  
   - Prefer a slightly warm cream over cool white; if it’s too pale, a tiny touch of yellow/ochre tint helps.  
   - Apply thin coats. Blend slightly past the repair so the edge disappears. Allow full dry time between coats.  
   - Woodgrain won’t fully return with paint; aim for an even cream tone that doesn’t catch the eye.

7. **Optional clear seal**  
   Some systems include a clear UV topcoat for outdoor-facing frames. Indoor sill-side repairs often only need the colour coat.

### What “good enough” looks like

- No sticky or soft filler
- Contour follows the original moulding
- Colour close to the clean beige in daylight (cream is harder than white; a soft blend beats a hard white patch)

---

## When to call a pro / replace more than the bead

- Burn goes into a hollow chamber and the frame feels soft or cracked beyond the pit
- Multiple burns or a large melted area affecting strength or weather seal
- Glass seal/gasket is damaged or misting appears between panes (separate issue — sealed unit)
- Rental / warranty: document with photos; some installers will replace a bead or section under goodwill

A single small sill-side melt like this is almost always a DIY fill or bead swap, not a full window replacement.

---

## Prevent it happening again

Sun + curved glass, garden sphere, stainless bowl, or **mirror on the sill** can focus enough heat to melt uPVC in minutes.

- Do not leave mirrors, magnifying makeup mirrors, or reflective ornaments on the sill in direct sun
- Close reflective blinds / move shiny objects when the sun tracks across that window
- If it happens again on the same elevation, consider a matte sill cover or relocating reflective items permanently

## Quick shopping list (Option B)

- [ ] IPA or uPVC cleaner  
- [ ] Wet/dry sandpaper 180 / 320 / 400  
- [ ] uPVC / PVC repair filler (two-part preferred)  
- [ ] Cream / beige / ivory uPVC touch-up paint or repair stick (not brilliant white)  
- [ ] Daylight photo or clean frame corner for colour matching in store  
- [ ] Masking tape, craft knife, cloths  

## Safety

- Work with ventilation; cured uPVC dust and solvent paints need care  
- Keep filler and paint off the glass gasket; residue can stain or stick  
- Don’t use open flame or high-temp heat guns near the frame unless doing a controlled plastic weld with the right kit
